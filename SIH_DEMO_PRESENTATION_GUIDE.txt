4. TOP 5 TOUGH QUESTIONS BY SIH JURY & WINNING ANSWERS
================================================================================

Q1: "Indian Railways has strict safety rules. How can Civil tampers and
     Electrical OHE crews work on the same track at the same time without accidents?"
A1: "Safety is our first constraint, not an afterthought. In Indian Railways
     General Rules (GR), heavy track machines like Ballast Cleaning Machines (BCM)
     infringe overhead wire electrical clearance. RailSync enforces deterministic
     safety interlocks: an electrical isolation power-cut block MUST precede the
     civil tamping block before the solver allows them to bundle. If spatial
     distance between crews is less than safety limits, bundling is strictly rejected."

Q2: "What happens in real operations when a train runs 45 minutes late?"
A2: "In today's system, when a train runs late, controllers scramble on phone calls
     and often cancel the maintenance block completely, wasting crew mobilization.
     Because RailSync's solver runs in just 1.8 seconds, section controllers can
     click 'Update plan', input the new timetable gap from COA, and RailSync
     re-optimizes the remaining schedule dynamically."

Q3: "How does RailSync integrate into existing Indian Railways software systems?"
A3: "RailSync acts as a microservice intelligence layer between existing silos:
     1. COA (Control Office Application) - pulls real-time train timetable gaps.
     2. TMS (Track Management System) - pulls pending civil track work orders.
     3. CMS (Crew Management System) - checks crew availability and rest hours.
     The output is sent as standard XML/JSON block requisitions directly into
     the Division Operating Manager's block approval terminal."

Q4: "Why is this an NP-hard problem? Can't someone just use Excel or linear math?"
A4: "With 35 tasks, 21 time slots, and 15 crew competencies, the permutation
     space is 35 x 21 x 15 = over 1.4 x 10^14 possibilities. In addition, real
     railways have non-linear spatial constraints: track machines travel at 30 km/h,
     crews cannot exceed 8-hour duty shifts, and power cuts must follow feeder
     sequences. Excel formulas cannot solve combinatorial explosion; RailSync uses
     branch-and-bound constraint pruning to solve it in 1.8 seconds."

Q5: "What if one department takes longer than planned and overstays their block?"
A5: "RailSync establishes a 'Master Block Window' based on the longest primary task,
     while shadow tasks operate inside with safety buffer margins (15 to 30 mins
     before line reopening). If a telemetry sensor or supervisor flags an overstay
     risk, the controller receives an alert 30 minutes in advance to execute
     standard track restoration protocols."
